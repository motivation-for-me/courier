# Assumptions and Open Decisions

This file records decisions that are not supplied by the client specification. They must be confirmed or refined before implementation reaches the affected area.

## Current Assumptions

1. The current repository is a visual-reference-only starting point. There is no existing frontend/backend infrastructure to preserve.
2. The target stack is Next.js/TypeScript/Tailwind for web, NestJS/TypeScript for API, Supabase PostgreSQL with Prisma, Docker Compose for non-database local dependencies, S3-compatible object storage, and Redis/BullMQ only when justified.
3. The first implementation is a modular monolith. Microservices are deferred until a measurable technical need exists.
4. Internal IDs will use opaque UUIDs. Public CN and manifest identifiers will use separate collision-safe sequences or allocation records.
5. A deployment may operate multiple organizations, branches, hubs, regions, and customer accounts. Scope is tenant-aware and server-enforced.
6. The UI examples use AED, UAE-style phone/address examples, and labels such as DXB/AUH; currency, locale, tax, timezone, service zones, and numbering formats must be configurable per organization.
7. The canonical status names in the Part 1 request are the initial domain vocabulary. Final exception and hold transition policies require operations sign-off.
8. Delivery completion requires OTP and COD/payment verification according to organization policy; the precise policy for prepaid, account, partial COD, and customer exceptions is pending.
9. Manifest receiving allows expected, received, missing, excess/unlisted, and diverted outcomes; discrepancy resolution authority and tolerance rules require confirmation.
10. Return/RTO creates a separate return record and does not mutate away original shipment history.
11. Customer portal access, external shipper APIs, notification providers, map/route providers, barcode symbologies, printer protocols, and identity-provider integration are future integration decisions.
12. PDF templates, legal retention, financial reconciliation periods, privacy retention, data residency, and document signatures require business and compliance decisions.
13. Password/session authentication is the baseline. Enterprise SSO, MFA requirements, device enrollment, and rider offline credential behavior are not yet specified.
14. Soft deletion will apply only to suitable reference data; operational, financial, tracking, delivery, audit, and document records are retained according to policy.
15. Generated documents are authoritative only when produced or approved by the backend; frontend previews are not authoritative.

## Questions to Resolve Before Affected Implementation

- What is the exact CN format and allocation scope: organization, branch, or global network?
- Which statuses can be manually overridden, by which roles, and with what approval evidence?
- What are maximum delivery attempts, retry windows, RTO triggers, and customer reschedule rules?
- Is OTP generated by the platform, an external provider, or both? What are expiry, retry, and fallback rules?
- Which payment methods and currencies are supported at launch, and what constitutes verification evidence?
- How are partial COD, refunds, failed collections, cash variance, and settlement adjustments handled?
- Which manifest discrepancy outcomes block dispatch/reconciliation, and who can resolve them?
- What personal-data and financial-data retention periods apply by jurisdiction?
- Which files may be uploaded, what malware scanning service is available, and what maximum sizes are acceptable?
- What reporting/export volume requires asynchronous jobs?
- Which notification channels and templates are required for booking, transit, delivery, failure, OTP, payment, and returns?

Unresolved items must become explicit policy/configuration or documented implementation decisions before the relevant module is considered production-ready.
